The constants of nature

3

What it is: how many independent directions space has. We live in three: left–right, forward–back, up–down.

What it controls: how forces like gravity spread out. Picture gravity as a fixed number of lines coming out of a star; its strength is how crowded those lines are. Farther away, the same lines cover a bigger surface. In 3D that surface is a sphere, so twice as far means ¼ the strength.

If you change it: with more dimensions, forces thin out faster with distance; with fewer, there is less room to build anything complicated. Try it and see what breaks.

Caveat for class: this is a toy. Each threshold is a rough rule of thumb taken from the fine-tuning literature (Rees's Just Six Numbers, Barrow & Tipler, Weinberg on Λ, Oberhummer et al. and Epelbaum et al. on carbon production in stars, Carter on star types, Carr & Rees on star lifetimes, Hogan on quark masses, Adams on stars in other universes). Real effects interact. Several are actively disputed. The structure animation is a simple model of how gravity gathers matter (the Zel'dovich approximation), drawn the way astronomers draw computer simulations of the cosmic web.
