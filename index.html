The constants of nature

3

What it is: how many independent directions space has. We live in three: up–down, left–right, forward–back.

What it controls: how forces spread out. In 3D, gravity and electric forces weaken as 1/r², which is exactly what allows stable planetary orbits and stable electron orbitals in atoms (Ehrenfest, 1917).

If you change it: in 4D, forces fall as 1/r³ and any orbit either spirals in or flies off. In 2D, there's too little room for complex structures; networks like nerves can't cross without intersecting.

Big question: string theory suggests extra dimensions might exist but be curled up tiny. Why are exactly three large?

Caveat for class: this is a toy. Each threshold is a rough rule of thumb taken from the fine-tuning literature (Rees's Just Six Numbers, Barrow & Tipler, Weinberg on Λ, Oberhummer et al. and Epelbaum et al. on the Hoyle state, Carter on convective stars, Hogan on quark masses, Adams on stars in other universes). Real effects interact. Several are actively disputed; Adams, for example, argues stars are possible across a large share of parameter space. Changing one knob at a time also overstates the fine-tuning, which is itself a good discussion point.
